T4 · Webex Contact Center Enterprise

WxCC Enterprise Overview

Webex Contact Center Enterprise is a cloud contact center for large enterprises. It is a different product from multi-tenant Webex Contact Center, and a different product from on-premises Unified CCE and Packaged CCE. This room keeps those names apart.

Room T4-R01 Version v0.1 last-verified: TODO Difficulty L2 Est. time 70–90 min Prereq T3-R01 Reading 18 min

Key takeaways

  • Dedicated Instance’s contact-center article calls Webex Contact Center Enterprise “a Cloud contact center for large enterprises” and points at a data sheet for details. [cite:1]
  • The same article lists UCCX, UCCE, PCCE, Webex CCE, and WxCC as different deployment models. It sends UCCE and PCCE readers to Cisco Unified Contact Center Enterprise component documentation. [cite:1]
  • The Quick Deployment Solution is one offer: 500 to 24,000 concurrent agents, initial solution in the U.S. only, with agents also in the U.S. [cite:2]
  • That offer’s day-1 Standard license is inbound and outbound voice, a browser desktop, and web and voice callbacks. Premium adds email, web chat, multi-channel reporting, and supervisor monitoring and barge-in. [cite:2]

03 · Core concepts

What the articles actually separate

Webex CCE

In the Dedicated Instance contact-center article, Webex Contact Center Enterprise (Webex CCE) is the cloud contact center for large enterprises. The data sheet is the pointer for further detail. Read scale on the Enterprise data sheet Cisco links from the help article. This room leaves the figures on that sheet because they change when Cisco revises it. [cite:1]

WxCC

Webex Contact Center (WxCC) is the separate SaaS name in that same list. Its architecture article describes the multi-tenant cloud: AWS, Kubernetes, entry points, flows, queues. That description is T3, not this room. [cite:1] [cite:4]

UCCE and PCCE

Unified Contact Center Enterprise and Packaged Contact Center Enterprise are listed beside Webex CCE, not inside it. The article tells you to read Cisco Unified Contact Center Enterprise and component documentation for those products. [cite:1]

Do not inherit the node list

Router, Logger, CVP, and Finesse are the usual on-premises vocabulary. They are not restated as components of cloud Webex CCE in the help articles read for this room. Open the Enterprise data sheet and the component guide side by side. The help articles read for this room do not say the data sheet names Router, Logger, CVP, or Finesse, and the sheet changes on its own revision.

Cisco Live framing

PSOCCT-1014 (2024) and BRKCCT-2031 (2025) pair “Webex Contact Center Enterprise (Dedicated Instance Cloud)” with “Webex Contact Center (Multi-tenant Cloud).” Agent-count bands on the 2025 deck are not design limits until a current data sheet maps them. [cite:5] [cite:6] re-check

05 · Flow

How a quick deployment is allowed to start

  1. Name the product. If the paperwork says Webex Contact Center without “Enterprise,” stop and open T3. [cite:1]
  2. If the paperwork is the Quick Deployment Solution, check geography: initial solution in the U.S. only, agents in the U.S. [cite:2]
  3. Check the agent license. Standard day 1 is voice, browser desktop, web and voice callbacks. Premium adds the digital and supervisor list. [cite:2]
  4. Refuse add-ons in that initial cut. Custom work goes through A2Q and lengthens the timeline. The page’s “as little as 10 days” assumes no customizations. [cite:2]
  5. Do not connect existing on-premises contact center in that initial deployment. The page says interconnection can come after go-live. [cite:2]
  6. If the story is the ny65x1bb customer, keep the counts: 20,000 users, 5,000 of them agents. Back office moves to Dedicated Instance. Agents move to WxCCE. Both use a Local Gateway for PSTN. Edge Connect 3.0 is a single peer to both. Inbound media flows from the LGW to the agent. After a transfer, media flows from the LGW to the back-office user. [cite:3]

09 · Self-question ladder

Twelve rungs

L1What one sentence defines Webex Contact Center Enterprise in the DI article?

A cloud contact center for large enterprises. Details are deferred to the data sheet, which this room does not invent. [cite:1]

L1Which names does that article refuse to merge?

UCCX, UCCE, PCCE, Webex CCE, and WxCC. [cite:1]

L2Who is the Quick Deployment Solution for, geographically, at the start?

The initial solution is available in the U.S. only, and agents must also be in the U.S. [cite:2]

L2What does Standard include on day 1 of that offer?

Inbound and outbound voice, a browser-based agent desktop, and web and voice callbacks. [cite:2]

L2What does Premium add that Standard’s day-1 list does not?

Email and web chat, multi-channel reporting and analytics, and supervisor monitoring and barge-in. DTMF IVR, Virtual Assistant–Voice, and nailed-up mobile agent are day-1 items for the offer, not Premium-only. [cite:2]

L2Where do customizations go?

Assurance to Quality (A2Q). They are not part of the initial quick deployment, and they add time. [cite:2]

L3Can the first cut trunk to an existing on-premises contact center?

Not in the initial deployment. The page says that interconnection can be added after go-live. [cite:2]

L3What is the 5,000-agent figure?

Part of one U.S. customer story: 20,000 users, 5,000 of whom are call-center agents. Not a published product maximum. [cite:3]

L3What media path does that example describe?

Both Dedicated Instance and WxCCE use a Local Gateway for PSTN. Inbound media flows from the LGW to the agent. After the agent transfers, media flows from the LGW to the Dedicated Instance back-office user. Edge Connect 3.0 can be a single peer to both. [cite:3]

L3Why is utqcm7 the wrong architecture page?

It is the Webex Contact Center architecture article used in T3. The DI article treats WxCC and Webex CCE as different models. [cite:4] [cite:1]

L4Which WFO article names Enterprise on purpose?

py6u9h, scoped to Webex Contact Center Enterprise, Contact Center Enterprise, and Contact Center Express. [cite:7]

L4What will you not claim about Router, Logger, CVP, or Finesse?

That they are components of cloud Webex CCE. The articles read here do not say that. Read the component names on the Enterprise data sheet itself. The help articles read here do not say what that sheet lists, and the sheet changes on its own revision. [cite:1]

13 · Interview

90-second answer

“Webex Contact Center Enterprise is Cisco’s cloud contact center for large enterprises. I do not use that name for multi-tenant Webex Contact Center, and I do not use it for on-premises UCCE or PCCE. Those are separate deployment models in the Dedicated Instance contact-center article. If the project is the Quick Deployment Solution, I stay inside that offer: 500 to 24,000 concurrent agents, U.S. only at the start, Standard voice versus Premium digital and supervisor tools, no custom gadgets and no on-prem interconnect in the initial cut. A story about 20,000 users and 5,000 agents, with back office on Dedicated Instance and agents on Enterprise, is one customer example: Local Gateway for PSTN on both sides, one Edge Connect peer, inbound media from the gateway to the agent, and after transfer from the gateway to the back-office user. It is not a cap. I will not invent Router, Logger, or Flow Designer as the cloud component list until a page I can cite says so.”
