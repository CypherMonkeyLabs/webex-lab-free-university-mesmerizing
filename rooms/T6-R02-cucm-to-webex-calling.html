T6 · Migrations

CUCM to Webex Calling

The documented path is Unified CM to multi-tenant Webex Calling. It is a PPDIO project, not a lift-and-shift. Phased moves split users across both platforms, and every group changes dial plans on Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateway.

Room T6-R02 Version v0.1 last-verified: TODO Difficulty L2 Est. time 90–110 min Prereq T2-R02 Reading 17 min

Key takeaways

  • The introduction says not to lift-and-shift Unified CM. Redefine requirements. The method is PPDIO: Prepare, Plan, Design, Implement, Optimize. [cite:1]
  • Choose flash-cut or phased. Flash-cut moves everyone at once. The guides spend their detail on phased, because groups of users sit on both platforms during the move. [cite:1] [cite:3]
  • Each time a group moves, dial plans and call routing must be updated on Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateway. [cite:3]
  • Cisco recommends migrating a physical site or department together, unless feature dependencies force smaller batches (shared hunt pilot, call queue, shared line, pickup, call park). [cite:4]

01 · Executive summary

Five lines, then why it matters

  • The introduction says not to lift-and-shift Unified CM. Redefine requirements. The method is PPDIO: Prepare, Plan, Design, Implement, Optimize. [cite:1]
  • Choose flash-cut or phased. Flash-cut moves everyone at once. The guides spend their detail on phased, because groups of users sit on both platforms during the move. [cite:1] [cite:3]
  • Each time a group moves, dial plans and call routing must be updated on Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateway. [cite:3]
  • Cisco recommends migrating a physical site or department together, unless feature dependencies force smaller batches (shared hunt pilot, call queue, shared line, pickup, call park). [cite:4]
  • BroadWorks and BroadCloud have a separate Control Hub migration. They are not this Unified CM path. [cite:9]
Why it matters. Cutover week fails when the dial plan is updated on only one of the three systems, or when a hunt group is split across the move. The lab on the Labs page is a study model of +E.164 normalization, not a CUCM engine.

03 · Core concepts

Decisions the introduction forces

PPDIO

Prepare, Plan, Design, Implement, Optimize. The introduction uses that sequence for Unified CM to Webex Calling and says the current deployment should not be lifted as-is. [cite:1]

Flash-cut versus phased

Flash-cut moves users and devices at once and is the fastest. Phased moves groups over time. While that happens, enterprise users are split across Unified CM and Webex Calling. [cite:1] [cite:3]

Reasons to consider staying

The introduction lists unsupported phone models; complex on-premises integrations that are hard to replicate; a complex dial plan, highly granular classes of service, or both; restrictive or unreliable internet; stringent data-privacy policies; compliance that needs on-premises or in-country recording; third-party integrations without a Webex alternative; and contact-center integrations where the contact center is not moving to the cloud yet. The last bullet is clipped on the page at the word “inter.” [cite:1]

+E.164

The Webex Calling configuration workflow says the Unified CM dial plan normalizes numbers as +E.164. Webex Calling sends unmatched calls, including PSTN numbers and Unified CM extensions it cannot see, to the Local Gateway. [cite:8]

CSS order, from the System Guide

The migration articles still only say to map the dial plan. The evaluation rule is in the Cisco CallManager System Guide, Release 4.0(1), chapter “Partitions and Calling Search Spaces,” and the 12.5(1) Troubleshooting Guide restates it. Digit analysis looks through every partition in the calling search space for the best match. Partition order breaks ties when two patterns match equally. The null partition is searched last. A line CSS and a device CSS are concatenated, line first. The Labs walker now follows that rule. It is not a live CUCM. [cite:10] [cite:11]

04 · Ecosystem

What has to move with the users

PPDIO
Prepare, Plan, Design, Implement, Optimize. The migration introduction’s process. [cite:1]
Flash-cut
Move all users and devices at once. Fastest, and the introduction contrasts it with a phased approach. [cite:1]
Phased migration
Groups move over time. Users are split across Unified CM and Webex Calling until phase 2. [cite:3]
Local Gateway
For a phased move the introduction names ISR 1100 and 4000, Catalyst 8200 and 8300, Catalyst 8000V, and certified third-party SBCs. [cite:1]
Unmatched call
A destination Webex Calling does not own. The configuration workflow sends those calls, including unseen Unified CM extensions, to the Local Gateway. [cite:8]
+E.164
The form the configuration workflow attributes to Unified CM normalization. [cite:8]
Main number
Set for each location. It can belong to a user or to a feature such as an auto attendant. [cite:4]
Migration Insights
Control Hub path Services → Upgrades & Migrations → Migration Insights. Unified CM data can be uploaded as a .tar. [cite:5]
Migrate features from UCM
Control Hub card under Services → Updates & Migrations. Maps auto attendant, devices, lines, layout, virtual line, call queue, hunt group, call park, and user calling data. [cite:6]
UserBasedUCMFeatureMigration
Python script that processes Unified CM CDR for migration tasks. Read what the feature-migration article says the script does, then run the published tool. The article does not print the Python internals, and they change when Cisco ships a new script. [cite:6]
Enterprise to MPP
7800 and 8800 phones can be moved with Control Hub’s “Migrate your phone to Webex Calling” tool. 8821 and 8831 are not eligible for MPP. [cite:4] [cite:7]
BroadWorks migration
A different Control Hub tool. PBX type BroadWorks or BroadCloud. The do_not_move_phone flag is BroadWorks-only. [cite:9]

05 · Flow

Order of the move

  1. Prepare: decide whether the requirements say to stay. Unsupported phones, a complex dial plan or highly granular classes of service, and a contact center that is not moving are on that list. [cite:1]
  2. Provision users in Webex even if the cut is phased. Confirm desk phones are in the 6800, 7800, 8800, or 9800 series. [cite:2]
  3. Plan Jabber: small populations can move client and calling together. Larger populations should move the soft client first, with Webex App still registered to Unified CM, and move calling in a second step. [cite:3]
  4. Choose groups. Prefer a physical site or department. Shrink the batch only for feature dependencies. [cite:4]
  5. For each group, update dial plans and call routing on Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateways. [cite:3]
  6. Set each location’s main number. Move voicemail, auto attendants, call queues, and hunt groups with the users who depend on them. [cite:4]
  7. Import Unified CM data via Migration Insights (.tar, then Step 3, Start New Task). Import mapped features from the Updates & Migrations card. Use the CDR script only as that article describes it. [cite:5] [cite:6]
  8. Migrate eligible 7800 and 8800 phones to MPP with the Control Hub tool. Exclude 8821 and 8831. Phones the firmware tool migrates must use Webex Calling. [cite:7]
  9. After the last group (the plan article’s phase 2), on-premises UC can be removed. Not before. [cite:3]

09 · Self-question ladder

L1What does the introduction forbid as a method?

A lift-and-shift of the current Unified CM deployment. Redefine requirements. [cite:1]

L1What does PPDIO stand for in this guide?

Prepare, Plan, Design, Implement, Optimize. [cite:1]

L2When are users split across both platforms?

During a phased move, until everyone has transitioned. [cite:3]

L2Which three places need dial-plan updates for each group?

Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateways. [cite:3]

L2What does Webex Calling do with a Unified CM extension it cannot see?

It sends the call to the Local Gateway, along with other unmatched destinations such as PSTN numbers. [cite:8]

L2How does the configuration workflow describe Unified CM numbering?

The Unified CM dial plan normalizes numbers as +E.164. [cite:8]

L3Who should use dual-step Jabber migration?

Larger customers. Step 1 moves Jabber to the Webex App while the app still registers to Unified CM. Step 2 moves calling. Single-step is the small-population option. [cite:3]

L3What batching does Cisco recommend, and what overrides it?

Physical sites or departments together. Feature dependencies such as a shared hunt pilot, call queue, shared line, pickup, or call park can force a tighter batch. [cite:4]

L3Which 8800 models are not eligible for MPP?

8821 and 8831. 8832NR can migrate with a feature caveat. V15 and later need at least 11.3.3 MPP and cannot downgrade. [cite:7]

L3Where do you import a Unified CM .tar?

Control Hub → Services → Upgrades & Migrations → Migration Insights, then Step 3 Migrate to Webex Calling → Start New Task. [cite:5]

L4Name two reasons the introduction gives for considering staying on premises.

Any two from the printed list. A complex dial plan or highly granular classes of service is one. A contact center that is not moving yet is another. [cite:1]

L4What will you not claim about CSS order?

An evaluation algorithm. It is not in these articles. The labs explorer is a teaching model. Read closest-match on the Unified CM that still routes the call. The labs explorer is a teaching model, and it is not the order that cluster uses. [cite:1]

13 · Interview

“I treat Unified CM to Webex Calling as PPDIO, not a copy of the old dial plan. If we phase it, every group is a routing change on Unified CM, Webex Calling, and the Local Gateway, because Webex Calling sends extensions it cannot see to that gateway. I move a site or a department together, and I only shrink the batch when people share a hunt pilot, queue, line, pickup, or park. Jabber on a large site moves to the Webex App while it still registers to Unified CM, and calling moves second. 8821 and 8831 do not go through the MPP tool. I will not pretend a CSS crosswalk exists, and I will not decommission Unified CM until the last user is gone. BroadWorks is a different article.”
