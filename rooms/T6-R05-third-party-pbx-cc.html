00 · Header

T6 · Migration

Third-party PBX & CC to Webex

Cisco names a third-party PBX as a source the Dedicated Instance method has covered, and it names third-party phones as a provisioning case. It does not print an Avaya, Genesys, or Amazon Connect object crosswalk. What it does print is how Webex Contact Center reaches a premises PBX or Microsoft Teams, and how UCCX may sit beside Dedicated Instance.

Room T6-R05 Version v0.1 last-verified: TODO Difficulty L4 Est. time 90–110 min Prereq T6-R04 Reading 18 min

Key takeaways

  • The Dedicated Instance practice article lists third-party PBX among sources the method has been used for. It does not print the object map. [cite:1]
  • DeviceEligibility.csv identifies third-party devices. Many non-phone devices have no enterprise-to-cloud firmware path, so you provision them in Control Hub. [cite:2]
  • Webex Contact Center can deliver to a premises PBX or to Microsoft Teams through a Local Gateway Direct SIP trunk. Cisco does not support connecting Webex Calling PSTN calls directly to Microsoft Teams endpoints. [cite:3]
  • A dial number is free-form. +E.164 is typical and not mandatory. The number must be reachable. [cite:3]
  • On-premises UCCX talking to Unified CM inside Dedicated Instance needs round-trip time under 60 ms. Dedicated Instance to Webex Contact Center uses certificate trunks. [cite:4]

01 · Executive summary

Five lines, then why it matters

  • Migration Insights reads a Unified CM tar. A third-party PBX export is not that tar. [cite:2] [cite:5]
  • The implement article says to work with the third-party vendor for third-party devices and phones. [cite:5]
  • Local Gateways such as Cisco CUBE or approved third-party SBCs can be set for +E.164 so they interoperate with an on-premises PBX. Session border controller configuration is the administrator’s work. The voice-channel article does not include vendor-specific SBC steps. [cite:3]
  • Microsoft Teams agents use a registration or certificate Local Gateway, CUBE or a third-party SBC, +E.164 on the gateway, and presence sync between Agent Desktop and the Teams client. [cite:3]
  • A customer story offers three contact-center approaches beside Dedicated Instance: Webex Contact Center, UCCX on Dedicated Instance IaaS, or UCCX left on premises if the 60 ms round-trip holds. [cite:6]
Why it matters. An interview that asks for an Avaya-to-Webex field map has to hear the gap. The cited pages name the source and the trunk. They do not name the object crosswalk.

06 · Config

Paths you can cite

  1. Read DeviceEligibility.csv from Migration Insights before you schedule a third-party phone. The report names IP phones, Room OS, ATAs, and third-party devices. [cite:2]
  2. For a device with no enterprise-to-cloud firmware path, add it in Control Hub. The guide’s replacement example is ATA 191/192. Work with the vendor when the implement article says to. [cite:2] [cite:5]
  3. On the Local Gateway, build a dial plan that matches the premises agent destination in E.164 and +E.164 if agents will sign in either way. [cite:3]
  4. For Microsoft Teams agents, use a registration or certificate Local Gateway and the CUBE direct-routing PDF, or the SBC vendor’s guide. Include trunks, route groups, and unknown-extension routing. [cite:3]
  5. For Dedicated Instance agents on Webex Contact Center, use the certificate inter-op trunk. Do not revive a registration trunk for that path. [cite:4]
  6. If UCCX stays on premises and Unified CM moves into Dedicated Instance, measure round-trip time and stop if it is not under 60 ms. [cite:4]
Warn. The voice-channel article says administrators configure the SBC from Control Hub parameters, and that detailed SBC steps are not in that article. Do not invent IOS lines here. [cite:3]

13 · Interview

Oral answers

30 seconds. Cisco says the Dedicated Instance method has been used from a third-party PBX. The migration tools still eat a Unified CM tar. Third-party phones are provisioned in Control Hub when they have no firmware conversion. There is no Avaya field map in the pages this room cites.

2 minutes. Webex Contact Center can send an agent call to a premises PBX, a DID, or a cell, through Cloud Connected PSTN or a Local Gateway. The gateway is CUBE or an approved SBC, and the administrator owns the SBC lines. A dial number is free-form. Plus E.164 is the usual form and is not mandatory, but the number has to be reachable. Microsoft Teams agents use Direct SIP on that same gateway class, with presence sync. Cisco does not support handing Webex Calling PSTN calls straight to Teams endpoints.

30 seconds, UCCX. If UCCX stays on premises and Unified CM moves into Dedicated Instance, JTAPI round-trip must be under 60 milliseconds. The integration article says most AMER premises links will miss that. The customer story’s other choices are Webex Contact Center, or UCCX bought separately and placed on Dedicated Instance IaaS.

2 minutes, trunks. Dedicated Instance into Webex Contact Center uses certificate trunks. Registration trunks are no longer the supported inter-op. Inbound can arrive on a Local Gateway into multi-tenant and then the contact center, or on Cloud Connected PSTN. Do not reuse the Calling acceptance phrase “round robin, longest idle, or simultaneous ring” as if it were the Webex Contact Center queue catalog.

What you will not say. You will not invent an Avaya, Genesys, or Amazon Connect crosswalk. You will not paste IOS from the CUBE PDF into an answer you have not opened. You will not call App Hub a migration tool.
