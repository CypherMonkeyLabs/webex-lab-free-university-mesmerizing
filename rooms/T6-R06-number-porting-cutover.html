00 · Header

T6 · Migration

Number Porting & Cutover

A port moves a number from the losing carrier to the winning carrier. For Cisco Calling Plans, Control Hub is where the order, the letter of agency, and the day-of checks live. The date is not real until the losing carrier returns a firm order commitment. Port-out uses a different menu than port-in.

Room T6-R06 Version v0.1 last-verified: TODO Difficulty L3 Est. time 80–100 min Prereq T6-R05 Reading 16 min

Key takeaways

  • One license can port one number. Ports cannot exceed licenses purchased. [cite:1]
  • Bring a bill from the last 30 days and a letter of agency. Some countries use an eLOA that Control Hub fills. [cite:1]
  • Do not disconnect the losing carrier until the port completes and you have verified it. After submit, numbers cannot be added or changed. [cite:1]
  • Before FOC you can change or cancel in Control Hub. After FOC you open PTS. Risk rises as the window shrinks. [cite:1]
  • A port-out request is auto-accepted after one business day. The menu is PSTN & Routing → PSTN Orders, which is not the port-in path Calling → PSTN → Orders. [cite:2] [cite:1]

01 · Executive summary

Five lines, then why it matters

  • Slamming is forbidden. Cisco does not change a telephony provider without written consent of the authorized account holder. [cite:1]
  • Same-account numbers port together even across orders. Distinct account information means separate orders. Toll-free is a separate order from DID, and the guidelines say US or CA only. [cite:1]
  • Ask for at least five business days, or two to three weeks for a larger port. The earliest request can be 1–35 business days. The latest is 30 days from submission. Activation is requested between 9 a.m. and 4 p.m. on business days and is not guaranteed until FOC. [cite:1]
  • Wait at least 15 minutes after FOC, or 30 minutes for a project or complex port, before you call the port failed. Then check Control Hub, then open PTS. [cite:1]
  • In the United States and Canada, porting a number ports Calling services. It does not port messaging. [cite:3]
Why it matters. Cutover day fails when someone disconnects the old carrier at submit, edits the order after FOC inside Control Hub, or treats a port-out menu as the port-in menu.

04 · Ecosystem & sister technologies

Glossary, map, sisters, edges

Glossary

Losing carrier
The current PSTN provider. The winning carrier is the provider that receives the number. [cite:1]
FOC
Firm order commitment. The losing carrier’s date and time. Requested dates are not confirmed until FOC. [cite:1]
LOA
Letter of agency authorizing Cisco to act for the customer. Some countries use an eLOA filled in Control Hub. [cite:1]
BTN
Billing telephone number. If it ports, a non-ported number is designated as the replacement. [cite:1]
Snapback
Emergency restore of a number to the losing carrier, inside a 1–48 hour window. [cite:1]
PTS
Cisco PSTN Technical Support for Calling Plan number orders, porting, and related questions. [cite:1]

06 · Config

Paths you can cite

  1. Enable the Cisco Calling Plan on the location before a port-in. [cite:3]
  2. Create the draft in Control Hub. Upload a bill from the last 30 days. Sign the LOA, or use the eLOA when the country uses one. [cite:1]
  3. Review every number. After submit, numbers cannot be added or changed. Toll-free is its own order. [cite:1]
  4. Assign the numbers to users or features before the FOC time. Leave them inactive until activation completes. [cite:1]
  5. On the day, wait 15 minutes, or 30 for project or complex. If they still fail, check orders and number status, then open PTS from Calling → PSTN → Orders. [cite:1]
  6. To port out, open PSTN & Routing → PSTN Orders. Accept, or reject with a reason. If you do nothing, the request is accepted after one business day. [cite:2]
Warn. Do not disconnect the losing carrier at submit time. The guidelines say the numbers stay active with that carrier until the port is complete and verified. [cite:1]

09 · Self-question ladder

Twelve rungs

L0What is a port?

A transfer from the losing carrier to the winning carrier. [cite:1]

L1What two documents prove you may port?

A bill from the last 30 days and an LOA or eLOA. [cite:1]

L1How many numbers does one license allow?

One. The port count cannot exceed purchased licenses. [cite:1]

L2When is the requested date real?

When the losing carrier returns an FOC. Until then it is a request. [cite:1]

L2What happens to numbers on the same account?

They port together, even if they were typed on different orders. [cite:1]

L3How do you prove you can still edit the order?

FOC has not arrived. After FOC, Control Hub edits stop and PTS is the path. [cite:1]

L3What evidence shows a port-out was ignored?

One business day passed on PSTN & Routing → PSTN Orders. The article says the request is then accepted automatically. [cite:2]

L4Why are there two menus?

Port-in support is Calling → PSTN → Orders. Port-out processing is PSTN & Routing → PSTN Orders. The articles print both. Do not collapse them. [cite:1] [cite:2]

L4What does a US port not move?

Messaging. Get Started says only Calling services port in the United States and Canada. [cite:3]

L5Which order-type cell will you not “fix”?

Complex is headed “Any of the following” and starts with “> 1 TN,” while Standard is 1–50 TNs. Quote both. Do not invent precedence. [cite:1]

L5What day count will you not invent?

How long “a reasonable period” is before unported numbers are released after Calling Plan cancellation. [cite:1]

L3When may you cancel the losing carrier?

After the port completes and you have verified it. [cite:1]

13 · Interview

Oral answers

30 seconds. Porting moves a number from the losing carrier to the winning carrier. Cisco will not do it without the authorized holder’s written consent. You need a bill from the last 30 days and an LOA. The date you type is a request until the losing carrier sends an FOC.

2 minutes. One license ports one number. Numbers on the same account move together. Toll-free is a separate order, and the guidelines limit that sentence to the US or Canada. Keep the old service up until you have verified the port. Before FOC you can still change the order in Control Hub. After FOC you open PSTN technical support, and the risk bands run from 72 hours down to under 24 hours. Snapback is an emergency restore inside 1 to 48 hours. On the day, wait 15 minutes, or 30 if the order is project or complex.

30 seconds, menus. A port-in support case starts at Calling, PSTN, Orders. A port-out is under PSTN and Routing, PSTN Orders, and it auto-accepts after one business day. If the location main number leaves, assign a new one.

2 minutes, what else moves. In the United States and Canada the port moves Calling, not messaging. Broadband or an alarm on that line can cease with the installation. A reseller bill is not enough if an underlying carrier owns the number. When the Calling Plan itself is cancelled, you port out to keep the numbers. Cisco does not promise you can get a released number back, and it does not print how many days “reasonable” is.

What you will not say. You will not merge the Complex column with Standard. You will not invent a Cisco Calling Plan country list. You will not treat Get Started’s “two business days” as a replacement for the guidelines’ 1–35 day range.
